01 · Core Idea

Core Idea

Secondary metabolites are the compounds responsible for the pharmaceutical and medicinal uses of plants. Their biosynthesis usually starts with primary metabolites (carbohydrates from photosynthesis) and follows two master routes: the shikimic acid pathway for aromatic compounds and the acetate–mevalonic acid pathway for aliphatic (nonaromatic) compounds. A mixed pathway derives from both.

02 · Learning Points

What you should leave with

  • Differentiate primary from secondary metabolites
  • Know the 5 classes of secondary metabolites and the example compounds
  • Secondary metabolites can be aromatic or aliphatic — each has its own pathway
  • Shikimic acid pathway: importance, intermediates, chorismic acid branch point, and the three aromatic amino acids
  • Acetate–mevalonate pathway: Acetyl-CoA → HMG-CoA → mevalonic acid → IPP/DMAP → terpene chain lengths (C10, C15, C20, C30)
  • How statins (antilipidemic drugs) block cholesterol formation
  • The master route linking photosynthesis to every class of secondary metabolite

05 · Important Concepts

Shikimic acid pathway — importance

  • Most medicinally active secondary metabolites are aromatic compounds
  • Produces the critical aromatic amino acids phenylalanine, tyrosine, tryptophan — synthetic precursors of many medicinal compounds
  • Biosynthetic precursor of aromatic amino acids, tannins, flavonoids, coumarins and others

Shikimic acid pathway — occurrence

  • Present and used by plants, fungi and bacteria; absent in animals
  • Animals cannot synthesize the three aromatic amino acids → they are essential nutrients in the animal diet
  • Starts from simple carbohydrates (primary) from glycolysis and the pentose phosphate pathway; ends with aromatic amino acids that form aromatic active compounds (secondary)

Acetate–mevalonate pathway (isoprenoid biosynthesis)

  • Biosynthesizes aliphatic active compounds: terpenoids and steroids
  • Forms the isoprenoid units, the building blocks of terpenoid and steroid biosynthesis
  • IPP and DMAP are the basic precursors of terpenoids
  • IPP is interconvertible with DMAP; DMAP is very reactive at the pyrophosphate position
  • IPP keeps substituting the pyrophosphate group to build terpenoids and steroids

Squalene → steroids

  • 2 Farnesyl-PP (C15 + C15) → Squalene (C30)
  • Oxidation + cyclization → cyclo-penta-phenanthrene, the basic skeleton of cholesterol and other steroids/triterpenoids

07 · Processes / Stages

Master route

  • CO₂ + H₂O + sunlight → photosynthesis → Carbohydrates
  • Carbohydrates → Pentose phosphate pathway → Erythrose-4-phosphate → Shikimic acid pathway → Flavonoids, volatile oils / aromatic amino acids → Alkaloids
  • Carbohydrates → Glycolysis → Phosphoenolpyruvate → Pyruvic acid → Acetyl-CoA
  • Acetyl-CoA → Mevalonic acid / isoprene unit (isoprenoid / acetate pathway) → Terpenoids, steroids, volatile oils
  • Acetyl-CoA → Aliphatic amino acids → Alkaloids

Shikimic acid pathway — sequence

  1. PEP + Erythrose-4-phosphate → 2-Keto-3-deoxy-7-phospho-D-glucoheptonic acid
  2. Cyclization + removal of phosphate → 3-Dehydroquinic acid
  3. Dehydration → 3-Dehydroshikimic acid
  4. Keto group replaced by OH (hydrogenation) → Shikimic acid
  5. Phosphorylation → Shikimic acid-3-phosphate
  6. + PEP, −H₂O → Chorismic acid (branch point)

Chorismic acid — two branches

  • Branch 1: → Anthranilic acid (amination and shifting) → + phosphoribosyl pyrophosphate + serine → Tryptophan
  • Branch 2: shifting rearrangement → Prephenic acid → phenylpyruvic acid → (reductive amination) Phenylalanine; or p-hydroxyphenylpyruvic acid → (reductive amination) Tyrosine

Acetate–mevalonate — sequence

  1. Glucose → 2 Acetyl-CoA → Aceto-acetyl-CoA
  2. + H₂O + another Acetyl-CoA (HMG-CoA synthase) → HMG-CoA
  3. HMG-CoA reductase (reduction) → Mevalonic acid
  4. Phosphorylation, then decarboxylation → IPP (C5)
  5. IPP ⇌ DMAP

10 · Clinical / Practical Importance

Why it matters

  • Secondary metabolites are responsible for the pharmaceutical and medicinal uses of plants
  • Statins (antilipidemic drugs) inhibit HMG-CoA reductase, blocking the conversion of HMG-CoA to mevalonate and so the formation of cholesterol
  • Animals lack the shikimate pathway → phenylalanine, tyrosine, tryptophan must come from the diet
11 · Exam Focus

Must remember

  • Primary vs. secondary: MW, quantity, role, distribution, examples
  • The 5 classes: Alkaloids, Flavonoids, Glycosides, Saponins, Tannins
  • Aromatic → shikimic acid; aliphatic → acetate–mevalonic acid; mixed = both
  • Shikimic acid first isolated in 1885 from the Japanese flower Shikimi
  • Shikimate pathway: plants, fungi, bacteria — not animals
  • Starting points: PEP (glycolysis) + erythrose-4-phosphate (pentose phosphate pathway)
  • Chorismic acid = most important intermediate; prephenic acid = last nonaromatic compound
  • Tryptophan via anthranilic acid; Phe via phenylpyruvic acid; Tyr via p-hydroxyphenylpyruvic acid
  • HMG-CoA synthase vs. HMG-CoA reductase (statin target)
  • IPP (C5) ⇌ DMAP; DMAP is reactive toward nucleophilic substitution
  • Chain lengths: C10 → C15 → C20; squalene C30 → steroids/triterpenoids
